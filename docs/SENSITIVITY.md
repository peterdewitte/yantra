# Decoder sensitivity (RAD-126)

How well the TETRA receiver copes with weak and impaired signals, measured
by the `yantra-sens` sweep: the generator (RAD-70) sends a cell, a channel
model and receiver impairments are applied, and the decoder's output is
compared with what was sent. The tables at the end are generated;
everything above them is the method and the reading of the results.
Copied from yantra 68ea20a: docs/SENSITIVITY.md, adapted.

## Running it

```sh
build/bin/yantra-sens --tier quick              # 4, 8, 12, 20 dB, one run of 4 s: about 1 s
build/bin/yantra-sens --tier full --csv out.csv # 0..30 dB, 8 runs of 6 s: under 2 minutes on 12 cores
build/bin/yantra-sens --scenario ht200 --snr 10,20,30 --runs 20
build/bin/yantra-sens --list                    # the scenarios
build/bin/yantra-sens --tier full --update-doc docs/SENSITIVITY.md  # rewrites the tables below
```

Other options: `--seed` (base seed, default 1), `--threads`, `--isa
scalar|simd`, `--markdown FILE`, `--baseline FILE` (check expectations,
exit code 1 on a failure; with `--scenario` only that scenario's, with
`--csv` the verdicts as CSV). Every point is deterministic for its seed,
scenario, SNR and run, whatever the thread count.

## Method

Code: `tools/yantra-sens` (library `yantra::sens`: `sweep.hpp`, the
receiver side and the phase noise model in `src/receive.*`).

- **Scenarios**: awgn; cfo2, cfo20, cfo50 (carrier 2, 20, 50 ppm off at
  390 MHz); offcentre (100 kHz off the centre of a 288 kHz capture);
  clock50 (sample clock 50 ppm fast); tu50, ht200; phasenoise (10 Hz
  Wiener linewidth); combined (TU50 + 2 ppm + clock 20 ppm + 3 Hz phase
  noise).
- **SNR** is the mean signal power over the noise power in the symbol-rate
  bandwidth (18 kHz), i.e. Es/N0; Eb/N0 is 3 dB lower. With fading it is
  the mean over the fades.
- **Receiver chain**: the 72 kHz channel goes straight to the
  `CarrierDecoder`; cfo20, cfo50 and offcentre generate a 288 kHz capture
  and run the TETRA channelizer tuned to the 6.25 kHz grid point nearest
  the carrier, as the carrier search does (its spectrum peak; the
  demodulator takes the rest).
- **Channel models**: the generator's propagation conditions of EN 300
  392-2 (`gen/include/yantra/gen/channel.hpp`, tested in `tests/radio/gen_sim`): static, TU50 (0 and
  5 us at -22.3 dB, 50 km/h) and HT200 (0 and 15 us at -8.6 dB, 200 km/h),
  independently Rayleigh-fading taps.
- **Each run** starts two frames before frame 18 of a random multiframe;
  the measurement window starts at 0.25 s, so losses after a late lock
  count.
- **Metrics** per point, summed over its runs: BSCH, SYSINFO, SCH/F, AACH
  decode rates (blocks with a good CRC, AACH: RM(30,14) decoded, over
  blocks sent; 100 % minus this is EN 300 392-2's message erasure rate);
  burst sync (slots with a training sequence found); slot CRC error rate;
  raw BER of a free-running demodulator with the decoder's settings; lock
  time (to the first BSCH); MER.

## Where the receiver stands

**Static channel.** AWGN, a carrier error of 2, 20 or 50 ppm, an
off-centre carrier, a 50 ppm sample-clock error and 10 Hz phase noise give
the same curve within about 0.5 dB: SYSINFO and SCH/F decode >= 99 % from
9 dB (100 % from 10 dB), about 96 % at 8 dB. The raw BER is that of ideal
differential detection of pi/4-DQPSK:

| Es/N0 dB | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ideal differential detection, BER % | 13.03 | 7.19 | 3.05 | 0.86 | 0.13 |
| this receiver (awgn, full tier), BER % | 13.8 | 7.6 | 3.2 | 0.88 | 0.13 |

**Fading.** TU50 reaches 90 % SCH/F at 18 dB and 99 % at 26 dB. HT200
reaches 90 % only from about 24 dB and stays below about 95 %: its 15 us
echo at -8.6 dB leaves an error floor (raw BER 1.5 % at 30 dB) that only
an equaliser removes (not done).

**Against EN 300 392-2.** The standard's reference sensitivity is defined
at antenna levels: the thermal noise in 18 kHz is -131.4 dBm, so a level
L dBm is an SNR of L + 131.4 - NF dB for a front-end noise figure NF.
With the commonly quoted mobile reference levels (static -112 dBm,
dynamic -103 dBm; to be checked against the standard) and an SDR noise
figure of 6 to 10 dB, that is about 9 to 13 dB static and 18 to 22 dB
dynamic: met for static, near for TU50, about 6 dB short for HT200.

## Baseline

`tests/radio/sens/baseline/quick.csv` and `tests/radio/sens/baseline/full.csv`:
`scenario,snr_db,metric,bound,limit` lines, each limit set from the value
measured on 2026-11-03 (written next to it) with a margin: rates measured
at 100 % must stay >= 99 %, static rates 4 to 6 points, fading rates 6
(full) to 12-17 (quick, one run) points below the measured ones, BER 1.35
to 2.5 times the measured value, lock time 200 ms (measured 128 ms).

- `test_sens` (label fast): the sweep library (a clean signal decoding
  completely and deterministically, phase-noise statistics, CSV,
  Markdown and baseline formats).
- `sens_quick_<scenario>` (label `sens`): the quick tier against
  `quick.csv`, one ctest per scenario; the verdicts go to
  `build/sens/quick_<scenario>.csv`. The `sens-quick` workflow runs
  `ctest --test-dir build -L sens` on demand.
- `sens_full` (labels slow, release, opt_in): the full tier against
  `full.csv` before a release; verdicts in `build/sens/full.csv`. Opt-in:
  not in the default run; `YANTRA_TESTS_OPT_IN=1 ctest --test-dir build
  -R sens_full` runs it (CONTRIBUTING.md).

A receiver change that moves a curve: run the full tier, explain the
change in the commit message and update the limits and this page on
purpose; never loosen a limit to hide a regression.
